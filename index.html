Program Overview

The Master of Science in Cybersecurity Engineering gives students the skills, knowledge and expertise to work in the rapidly growing field of cybersecurity and design, engineer, and architect cybersecurity technology and systems.

Cybersecurity has been and continues to be one of the most growth-oriented career fields in the computer science domain. Graduates of this program will be equipped with the theoretical and hands-on engineering expertise to solve complex cybersecurity problems affecting diverse enterprises worldwide. McKelvey launched the degree in 2018.

Before you start: two prerequisites

All students must have completed these courses (or equivalents at another institution), test out of them, or complete them at the start of the program:

  • CSE 1301 Introduction to Computer Science
  • CSE 2407 Data Structures and Algorithms
  1. TranscriptDocumented on your undergraduate transcript.
  2. Placement examSuccessfully test to place out of the course.

General Degree Requirements

  • GPA

    As per McKelvey School of Engineering guidelines, students must maintain a GPA of at least 2.70.

  • Grades

    Six units of CSE 7998 are graded Pass/No Pass. The remaining 24 units must be taken for a grade (not Pass/No Pass), and the grade in each course must be C– or better.

  • Independent study

    None of the 30 units may be independent study (CSE 4000 (CSE 400) or CSE 5999 (CSE 500)).

  • “N” courses

    Courses with an “N” designation do not count toward the master's degree.

  • Substitutions

    Students who have already taken core or elective courses specified by the program can, with departmental approval, substitute other courses that are suitably technical and appropriate to the degree program. Departmental approval requires justification and is evaluated with increasing stringency for each additional substitution.

Master's Capstone

Beginning in Fall 2025, students taking the project or thesis option register for CSE 7998 (Master's Capstone). CSE 7998 does not count toward the 30 required units until the student has successfully defended the project or thesis.

Option 1

6-unit master's project

Upon a successful defense, 6 units of CSE 7998 count toward your coursework at the CSE 5000 level.

or

Option 2

Master's thesis

Upon a successful defense, 6 units of CSE 7998 count toward your coursework at the CSE 5000 level.

Either way, a notation is added to your transcript showing which option you completed. CSE 7998 is graded Pass/No Pass each semester. A passing grade and a completed progress report are required in the first semester to continue the work in the final semester.

What goes in the progress report?

Near the end of the first semester of a two-semester thesis or project, the student submits a written progress report to the committee. The committee uses it to evaluate progress and to give the student feedback. The report should include:

  • A copy of the original scope and duration of the work to be performed (from the original proposal form). Contact the Graduate Coordinator to receive a copy of your original proposal form.
  • A discussion of any major modifications or changes to the scope of the work.
  • An overview of the work completed during the first semester.
  • A discussion of the work yet to be completed and a timeline for completion.

Submit it by email to all members of the committee at least one week before the last official day of classes for the semester.

What outcomes can the committee decide?

Once the report is submitted, the committee confers privately and decides on one of three outcomes. The department's Graduate Coordinator communicates the outcome to the student before the official end of the semester.

  • Continue without concern. The student is making consistent progress and is on track to finish on time.
  • Continue with concern. The student is making some progress but is falling behind. Without change, the student is not likely to finish successfully.
  • Should not continue. Progress is insufficient and the project or thesis should be terminated. The student will not be enrolled in the second semester's units.
What happens if a project or thesis is terminated?

Starting Fall 2024, Master's Project/Thesis students cannot receive an “N” grade for a terminated project. Contact the Graduate Coordinator as soon as possible with any questions about terminating a project or thesis.

FAQs

Who is this master's degree for?

Students who want to design, engineer and architect cybersecurity technology and systems. You'll need a programming foundation equivalent to CSE 1301 (Introduction to Computer Science) and CSE 2407 (Data Structures and Algorithms). If you're a working professional focused on managing security rather than engineering it, compare the Master of Cybersecurity Management.

How long does the program take?

The degree is 30 units. The 6-unit capstone is typically completed over two semesters. Full-time tuition covers 9–21 units per semester.

Can I study part-time?

Yes. Part-time applicants have their own deadlines (July 15 for fall, Dec. 1 for spring), need two letters of recommendation instead of three, and don't need the GRE. Part-time study is open to U.S. citizens, permanent residents and holders of an eligible visa status. Part-time master's tuition is $2,450 per unit. Working professionals focused on managing security rather than engineering it can also compare the part-time Master of Cybersecurity Management.

What coding skills do I need before applying?

The equivalent of CSE 1301 and CSE 2407. You can show them on your undergraduate transcript, test out of them, or take them at the start of the program.

Is the GRE required?

No. The GRE General Test is optional for full-time master's applicants and not required for part-time applicants. If you send scores, use ETS school code 6929.

Do I have to write a thesis?

No. The program requires 6 units of CSE 7998 Master's Capstone, and you choose either a 6-unit master's project or a master's thesis. Both are defended and noted on your transcript. See Master's Capstone.

Can I substitute a course I've already taken?

Yes, with departmental approval. If you've already taken a core or elective course, you can substitute another suitably technical course. Each additional substitution is evaluated more strictly.

How do I pay for the degree?

Admitted students can be considered for the Master's Excellence Scholarship (up to $16,000) with no separate application. Research assistantships, federal loans and Work-Study are also available. See Tuition & Financial Aid.
